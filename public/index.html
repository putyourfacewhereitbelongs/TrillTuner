      Room & wood watch
      
          
      — tell me your room
      
          
          
          
      Wood breathes: keep a solid-top acoustic near 45–55% humidity. Big swings in temp or humidity pull your guitar out of tune — and can crack the top.

        Phrase looper — five takes, locked to the tempo
        
          
        The classic floor-pedal trick, without the pedal, and without the usual fumble at the record button. Press Rec and the metronome does the counting: it starts the metronome for you, counts you in, starts recording on the downbeat, records exactly the number of bars you picked, and stops itself on the beat. Then it trims the dead air off both ends and crossfades the loop seam, so there is no gap, no click and nothing left to trim by hand. Un-tick auto-align to record free and let it read the tempo off your playing instead.
        
          
        
            
            
            
            
            
          
        
          
        Five takes, A–E — Take A is live. Bank a few passes, then switch the moment one stops sitting right: click a letter, press A–E, or use ←/→ on the take row. Recording overwrites the take you have selected; tick Overdub to stack a layer onto it instead.
        
          
          
        
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
          
        
          
        Empty — press Rec and play. The loop keeps going while you switch to CAGED or the bend lab.
        
          
        On the waveform: the faint vertical lines are bar boundaries at the grid tempo, and the shaded strip at the left is the seam crossfade. If the loop is on the grid, every bar line lands on the same place in your phrase every time round.
        
        
        
      
        

      
        
        
        
          
        Clean loop — the instrument on loop, with the room taken out
        
          
        Play along with the mic. When you press Stop, the hiss, hum and room noise are taken out of the take, and the vocal too if you ask, then it is trimmed and looped. With the metronome running, the loop is locked to its whole bars. Use Hearing to flip between the cleaned take and the original, so you can hear exactly what was removed. Nothing leaves your device.
        
          
        Vocal removal works from the stereo image. On one mic it cannot always tell a voice from a centred guitar, so it also thins the guitar. Leave it off unless the voice is the thing that has to go.
        
          
        
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
            
          
        
          
        Press Record and play. The room is taken out of whatever the mic hears.
        
        
        
      
        

      
        
        
        
          
        Drone — a root (and fifth) that never blinks
        
          
        Play scales, bends and chords against a held pitch. If you wander out of the key you will hear it immediately — the cheapest, most honest ear trainer there is.
        
          
        
            
            
            
            
            
            
          
        
        
        
      
        

      
        
        
        
          
        CAGED — five shapes that cover the whole neck
        
          
        Every major (and minor) chord is one of five open shapes, moved up the neck. Learn where each lives for a root and you can play that chord — and its scale box — anywhere. Tap a card to hear it.
        
          
        
            
            
          
        
          
        
        
        
      
        

      
        
        
        
          
        Natural harmonics — the nodes that ring by themselves
        
          
        Rest a finger lightly over the fretwire (don’t press down), pick, and lift. Fret 12 is the octave, 7 is an octave plus a fifth, 5 is two octaves. Tap a card to hear the target; press Listen and play the harmonic — it lights up when you hit it.
        
          
        
            
            
            Pick a string, then chime a harmonic.
          
        
          
        
        
        
      
        

      
        
        
        
          
        Intonation — set this string’s saddle
        
          
        Compare the open string to the 12th-fret octave. Sharp at the 12th → move the saddle back (away from the neck). Flat → move it forward. One saddle at a time, then re-check. The Care tab has a full six-string worksheet; this is the “do this string now” version.

        Bend lab — land the note, not “around the note”
        
          
        Play the unbent pitch first (that becomes the zero). Then bend toward the target. Green means you are within ±12 cents of a half-step, a whole-step or a third — the three bends that actually show up in songs.

        Plug a real guitar into these amps
        
        
        The test riffs on the Rig tab are a synthesized preview — a processed wave that is not your guitar. To hear your electric through the amp and pedals, run a real instrument cable into an audio interface, then into this app. Only the amped signal is sent to the speakers. The dry guitar and the preview riffs stay off.
        
        
        
          
          
        
        
        
        Not live yet. Headphones on before you start — a guitar into speakers in the same room will howl.
        
      
        
      
        
        
        The cable path
        
        
          
          
        1. Guitar → ¼″ instrument cable → audio interface instrument (Hi-Z) input. A ¼″ to 3.5 mm headphone adaptor is not an instrument input. It will be silent, noisy, or it can damage the jack.
          2. 
          
        2. Interface → this device over USB-C, USB, or Lightning (iRig HD, Focusrite Scarlett, Zoom, iRig 2, etc.). On a phone you usually need a USB-C OTG adaptor or the iRig’s own Lightning/USB-C plug.
          3. 
          
        3. Headphones in the interface (or in the phone). That is how you hear the amp without a feedback loop.
          4. 
          
        4. In Settings, pick that interface as the input if more than one device is listed.
          5. 
          
        5. Press Start live guitar above. The Rig’s monitor turns on, preview riffs mute, and only the processed chain (pedals → amp → cab) goes to the speakers.
          6. 
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        Why you were hearing a “preprocessed wave”
        
        
        The Rig’s Test chord / Test chug / Test lead buttons pluck a Karplus–Strong string model — a synthesized guitar — through the amp so you can audition a sound without plugging in. That is not your guitar. While live guitar is on, those buttons refuse to play, so the speaker only carries the mic/interface signal after the pedals and amp. The dry (unprocessed) input is never mixed into the speakers.
        
        
        If you still hear a dry guitar, it is coming from the room (the guitar itself) or from the interface’s hardware direct-monitor switch. Turn hardware direct-monitor off so you only hear the app’s amped sound.
        
      
        
      
        
        
        Phones, tablets, laptops
        
        
          
          
        • Laptop: any USB audio interface with an instrument pad / Hi-Z button. Plug in, select it in Settings, start live guitar.
          • 
          
        • Android: a USB-C audio interface that speaks USB Audio Class, plus OTG if the phone needs it. Grant microphone permission — Android treats the interface as a mic.
          • 
          
        • iPhone / iPad: iRig, iRig HD, or a Camera-adapter + class-compliant interface. Same permission prompt.
          • 
          
        • Gain: set the interface so a hard strum is strong but not clipping the input meter. The amp’s gain knob is for tone; the interface gain is for a clean recording of the pickup.
